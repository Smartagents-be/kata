It lands the first time an agent does in ten minutes something you had set a day aside for. The work is good, too. That is the part that stings.

The route was the agent's. The decision to take it was yours: you named the goal, you set the check that said yes, and you read what came back before it went anywhere. Nothing in that sequence happened on its own.

You are still the engineer

Implementation is the part that moved, and enablement argues where it moved to. What that leaves you doing is judgement, and judgement shows up nowhere at five o'clock. An afternoon of deciding looks like an afternoon of nothing.

It is not. The empty list, the expired tier, the amount that is exactly zero: an agent writes the happy path and leaves those out, and the person who names them is the reason the module holds up. Nobody watches that work happen.

Tell the feeling from the signal

One version of this is not a feeling at all. If you cannot follow the diff, that is a gap rather than impostor syndrome, and a gap has a fix: hand over smaller steps, ask the agent to walk you through the part you lost, or write the awkward class yourself. Accepting code you do not understand is how a codebase turns into somebody else's.

The feeling is the other case. You followed every line, you know why it works, and you still tell yourself anybody could have asked for that. Maybe. They did not, and the run that produced it is sitting in your session with your goal at the top of it.

Nobody has been doing this long

The tools this course is about are a couple of years old, and they changed twice while it was being written. Whoever sounds certain about them read something last week, the same as you. There is no decade of practice here to be behind on.

What does add up is what you wrote down. A CLAUDE.md that got sharper, a skill that stopped needing the same correction, an audit.md you can diff against last month. That is the experience, and it is in the repository rather than in how you feel about it on a Thursday.
