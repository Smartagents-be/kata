Everything the agent knew when it answered you was text in one window. That is what every unit before the hunt was about: what the text is measured in, what fills it, what it costs, who reads it and where its answers came from.

What it costs, and what to do about it

  • Everything is counted in tokens, and code is dearer than text. Ask in the language the model has read most of: fewer tokens for the same question, and a better answer back .
  • Every follow-up re-sends everything above it, and what the model spent on thinking stays in there too. Bundle what belongs together instead of asking three times .
  • A tool costs you by existing, called or not. Turn off the ones this task does not need .
  • Nothing in the window is marked stale, and the pile only grows. Clear when you change subject, at a seam you pick rather than the one compaction picks for you .
  • You typed almost none of the session, and all of it goes out again on every turn. You are paying for it either way, so ask everything you want about a piece of code while it is still in front of you .
  • The harness is in the request before your sentence is, and it caches the front of the pile. Keep that cache alive or start over on purpose, because an entry goes stale after about five minutes .
  • The tier multiplies all of it, at roughly one, three and five per token. Let the expensive model write the brief and a cheap one run it .
  • An answer sounds the same whether it read your file or filled the gap. Ask for the check rather than the conclusion: a test that goes red on an empty list is a different kind of answer .
  • Your five-hour window opens on your first message, not your first real question. Say hello five hours before your break and it runs right up to the moment you stand up .
