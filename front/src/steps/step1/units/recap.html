Everything in this step comes back to 1 request: what goes into it, what it costs, and where the answer comes from.

  • Text is counted in tokens, and the start of each request is cached. Paste only the part of a log that matters, and pick your model and effort before you begin .
  • Only your prompt carries what you want. Name the goal, the files, a limit with its reason and a check the agent can run, and bundle related changes into 1 request .
  • An unused tool can still take up context, and what a tool returns can carry instructions. Turn off what this task does not need, and read what a tool brought in .
  • Your CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md comes back every session, and everything in the context leaves your machine. Keep that file current, and keep secrets out of what the agent reads .
  • Everything in your session is sent again on every turn, and after compaction only a summary is left. Clear between unrelated tasks, and write what has to last to a file .
  • Each sub-agent costs tokens and starts without your conversation. Cut the task into parts you can check, and give each sub-agent what it needs to know.
  • Model choice sets the price and what the model can handle. Try a cheaper model on a well-defined task, and check the result .
  • An answer sounds the same whether the agent read your file or filled the gap. Send it to the docs for your version, ask for a test that can go red, and check a package exists before you add it .
  • Claude’s 5-hour and weekly limits are separate from the context. Check both before you start a long run .
  • On a company seat, Copilot draws AI credits from a shared monthly pool. Your admin can give each person a different limit. Reaching yours stops the agent mid-task, so check your usage before a long run .
