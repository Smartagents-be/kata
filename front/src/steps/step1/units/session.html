Everything earlier in this conversation. Your previous turns, the agent's replies, the system messages the harness slipped in, every tool call it made and usually the bulk of it: the tool results. A file it opened, a test run it executed, a stack trace you pasted. All of that stays in the transcript and gets re-sent with every message.

There is no running conversation on the other side. Each time you press enter, your new prompt is appended to everything that came before and the whole pile goes out as one call to the model. Turn twenty is not a short message. It is turn one through nineteen plus your sentence. That growing pile is the session.

This layer grows the whole time you work, and it is the one that disappears when you type /clear. It also explains why an agent can feel sharp at the start of a session and vague two hours later. The signal is still in there, buried under a hundred tool results.

Sessions are where the money goes

Since the whole session is re-sent every turn, its size is your price per message. Read three large files early and you keep paying for those three files on every question you ask afterwards, whether or not they still matter. Managing the session is not tidiness. It is cost control.

The flip side is the cheapest thing you can do. Once Catalog is in the window, asking a second and a third question about it costs almost nothing extra, because the expensive part is already loaded. Ask everything you want to know about a piece of code while it is in front of you, rather than clearing and paying to load it again tomorrow.

Do not read that as a licence to keep going forever. The same session that makes the second question cheap makes the fiftieth question expensive and vague. Work a subject out, then move on deliberately.

Fixed does not mean gone

Say you spend twenty turns chasing a failing test and you fix it. The fix is in the code, but the twenty turns are still in the window: the wrong diagnosis, the two edits you reverted, the error output from a version of the file that no longer exists. The model reads all of it on your next question and has no strong reason to believe the last version is the real one.

That is the entropy from the opening unit, and this is where you push back on it. When a problem is closed, clear the session and start the next one with what actually survived: the code on disk, which is now correct, plus a sentence about where you are. You lose the noise and keep the work.

Careful session management is most of what separates people who get good work out of an agent from people who fight it. Not a bigger model, not a longer prompt. Knowing what is in the window right now, and being willing to throw it away.

Watch your own session once. Ask the agent what it has read so far in this conversation, and check how much of that list still has anything to do with what you are doing now.
