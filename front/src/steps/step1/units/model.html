A provider ships a family rather than a model. They differ in what they cost per token, how fast they answer and how well each holds a long task together.

Cost

Price is per token, and the tiers are further apart than most people expect. Call the small one a single unit. The middle tier costs roughly 2 of those, the top tier roughly 4 Output runs about 5 times the price of input in every tier, so a model that answers at length costs more than the same model answering briefly.

The bottom row is not one of the 3. Providers also ship a frontier model, priced past the top tier and kept for work the top tier cannot hold together. It is a ceiling rather than a 4th tier.

These prices are a snapshot from October 2026, and they change. Check today’s prices and try a model on your own work before you pick one.

API vs subscription

The harness is where you choose how you pay. Hand it an API key and the provider bills you per token, at the rates above, for exactly what went up and what came back. Sign in with a subscription instead and the same usage comes off a plan you paid for before you started.

That choice also decides which models you can reach. A subscription buys you into 1 provider's own program, so you get their family and nothing else. A key is looser. Most open-source harnesses take one, and that is what lets you point the same tool at whichever provider's models you like.

That choice also decides which models you can reach. A Copilot seat is a subscription, but the picker behind it is not 1 provider's family: you choose the model per conversation, across vendors, and the 3 tiers in this unit turn up there under several names. A key is looser still. Most open-source harnesses take one, and that is what lets you point the same tool at whichever provider's models you like.

On an API key, every request adds to a bill. A subscription has limits instead, shared by every Claude app you use Watch out: if ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is set in your shell, Claude Code can bill that key instead of your plan.

The tokens are the same either way. What differs is how the bill reaches you. On a key, a long context is a number growing on an invoice while you work, and you can go and look at it A Copilot seat draws the same tokens out of a monthly allowance of AI credits, and 1 credit is $0.01. Every model has its own rate for input, output and cached tokens, so the same task costs a different number of credits on a different model. In IntelliJ, the chat shows under each turn how many credits it used. The allowance does not roll into next month. On a company seat, the credits of every seat go into one pool the whole company shares. Your admin decides how much of it you may use and can give each person or team a different limit. When you reach yours, the agent stops. Autocomplete is the part of Copilot you are not charged for. Everything the agent does is.

In a company the choice is rarely yours. Someone bought seats, or someone holds a key and watches what it spends. Worth finding out which, because it decides whether a long context is your problem or a line on somebody's report.

The 5-hour limit

Claude subscriptions have a usage allowance that resets every 5 hours. That is a plan rule, not the size of the context, and your harness shows when the next reset comes.

A weekly limit sits on top, and it can run out even when the 5-hour one has reset. Hit either and you wait, or buy extra usage if your plan allows it.

If the clock starts at your first message, start early: the reset then lands on your lunch break instead of in the middle of a task.

Plan your important work around the allowance you actually have A reset does not give back what you used, and starting early does not lift the weekly limit.

Speed

Smaller models usually answer faster, but how much faster varies. If a step runs 100 times a day, time it on real work.

A fast wrong answer costs another run to fix. Use the smaller model where your checks show it does the job.

Reasoning level

The prompt unit introduced effort: how hard a model works. The model sets what it can handle, and more effort does not turn a small model into a big one.

Or let it pick for you

You can also let the tool choose. In Copilot, pick Auto in the model picker: it chooses per prompt and gives 10% off your credits In Claude Code, opusplan lets Opus plan and Sonnet build.

A clear brief helps any model: the outcome, the inputs, the limits and a check. Let the bigger model write it if that makes it clearly better.
