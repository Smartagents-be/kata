You choose which model your agent uses.

A provider ships a family rather than a model. Its models differ in price per token, speed and how well they hold a long task together.

Cost

The tiers are further apart in price than most people expect. The middle tier costs roughly 20 times the small one, the top tier roughly 40 times Output costs 5 times input in every tier, so a long answer costs more than a short one from the same model.

API vs subscription

The harness is where you choose how you pay. With an API key, the provider bills every token you send and get back, at the rates above. Sign in with a subscription and the same usage comes off a plan you paid for in advance.

That choice also decides which models you can reach. A subscription gets you 1 provider's family and nothing else. A key is looser: most open-source harnesses take one, so you can point the same tool at any provider's models.

A subscription has limits instead of a bill, shared by every Claude app you use If ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is set in your shell, Claude Code can bill that key instead of your plan.

The tokens are the same either way. On a key, a long context is a number growing on an invoice you can check while you work A Copilot seat draws them from a monthly allowance of AI credits, at $0.01 a credit. Each model has its own rate for input, output and cached tokens, so the same task costs a different number of credits per model. In IntelliJ, the chat shows under each turn how many credits it used. Unused credits do not roll into next month.

On a company seat, every seat's credits go into one pool the whole company shares, and your admin can give each person or team a different limit on it. When you reach yours, the agent stops. Code completions and next edit suggestions cost no credits. Chat and everything the agent does cost credits.

In a company the choice is rarely yours: someone bought seats, or someone holds a key and watches its spend. Find out which. It decides whether a long context is your problem or a line on somebody's report.

The 5-hour limit

Claude subscriptions have a usage allowance that resets every 5 hours: a plan rule, not the size of the context, and your harness shows when the next reset comes. A weekly limit sits on top and can run out even after the 5-hour one has reset. Hit either and you wait, or pay for usage credits if your plan allows it.

If the clock starts at your first message, start early: the reset then lands on your lunch break instead of in the middle of a task.

Plan your important work around the allowance you actually have A reset does not give back what you used, and starting early does not lift the weekly limit.

Speed

Smaller models usually answer faster, but how much faster varies. If a step runs 100 times a day, time it on real work.

A fast wrong answer costs another run to fix. Use the smaller model where your checks show it does the job.

Reasoning level

The prompt unit introduced effort: how hard a model works. The model sets what it can handle, and more effort does not turn a small model into a big one.

Or let it pick for you

You can also let the tool choose. In Copilot, pick Auto in the model picker: it picks the model as you go and takes 10% off your credits on a paid plan In Claude Code, opusplan lets Opus plan and Sonnet build.

A clear brief helps any model: the outcome, the inputs, the limits and a check. Let the bigger model write it if that makes it clearly better.
