This kata teaches you to work with a coding agent, one small step at a time. It starts simple and each step leans on the one before it.

Three things have to be on your machine before any of this works: this repository cloned, a JDK 25 or newer with Maven on your path, and a coding agent you can run in a terminal. The last page of this step checks all three for you.

How to use this document

There are three ways to read these pages:

  • On your own. Work through the text and the exercises in order and learn it by yourself.
  • With a tutor who guides you through every step at the board.
  • As reference. Come back to a page once you have met the thing it describes at your own desk.

A few options sit behind the menu in the top right to configure this course. One of them throws your progress away: it forgets every flag you captured and every page marked done.

Set the assistant to the one you actually use. The pages then name the commands that apply to you, instead of the other product's.

How exercises work

During the exercises you will meet strings like {f1r5t-5t3p5}. A string in braces is a flag. You hunt them down and type them back into a box in your browser.

Hints

Every answer box carries a Hint button. Press it when you are stuck and it will help you out.

In class, ask your tutor before you reach for the Hint. They can see where the whole room is stuck and can steer you faster than a line of text.

How workshops work

A workshop is a board of flags: some are hidden in the code for you to hunt down, others are printed by a build once you have the project where the step wants it. What you find you type into the board, and it checks your answer in the browser. Steps 0, 1 and 2 each have one.

House rules

Only your agent hunts. Do not go reading the code yourself. Ask for what you want and let the agent go and get it.

Cut before you prompt. "Find the flags in this repo" comes back fast, confident and wrong. Every flag has its own way in.

One flag, one session. A hunt leaves a trail of dead ends behind it, and the next flag has to read past all of them. Start each one on a fresh session unless the step says otherwise .

Make it run the check. Worked out in prose, a flag is a plausible string with two letters wrong, said just as surely. Have the agent run the thing and print the answer instead. And when several candidates come back in the flag's exact shape, the pick is yours. The agent cannot tell. You can.

Price the hunt when it is over. A flag is a few characters long. Getting to it was not, and you paid for every turn on the way. Step 1 gives you the numbers to put on that. The next page is where the code those flags hide in lives, and the page after it is this step's board.
