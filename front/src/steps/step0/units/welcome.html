This kata teaches you to work with a coding agent, one small step at a time. It starts simple and each step leans on the one before it, so the order is as much the lesson as the words are.

Before step 1, take two minutes on how the pages themselves work: the ways you can read them, how the exercises grade, and how to get unstuck.

How to use this document

There are three ways to read these pages, and the same pages serve all three:

  • On your own. Work through the text and the exercises in order and learn it by yourself.
  • With a tutor who guides you through every step at the board.
  • As reference. Come back to a page once you have met the thing it describes at your own desk.

A few extra options sit behind the menu in the top right: your language, whether the notes show, and slides. Slides are not built yet. They are coming soon.

How exercises work

Most pages are prose. Some end with a question or an answer box. Every exercise in this kata grades right here in your browser, so it works whether or not anything is running behind the site.

You'll find codes written like {f1r5t-5t3p5} during the exercises. A string wrapped in {} is the signifier for a written exercise: it is the answer. These are to be hunted down and typed back into a box in your browser. This page has one, and its code is right there above. Put it in the box below and press Check.

Hints

Every answer box carries a Hint button. Press it and a short nudge opens, enough to get you moving again without handing you the answer outright.

Try it on the box below. This one does not print its code in the prose. Press its Hint button and the code shows up in the panel that opens. Read it there, then type it back into the box.

In class, ask your tutor before you reach for the Hint. They can see where the whole room is stuck and can steer you faster than a line of text.
