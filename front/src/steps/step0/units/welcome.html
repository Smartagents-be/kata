In this kata you learn to work with a coding agent. It starts simple, and every step adds new techniques.

Ask your agent to execute install.txt from the root of this repository. Your agent then checks your machine and sets up what the course needs.

Before you start, you need:

  • A local clone of this repository.
  • JDK 25 or newer, with Maven on your PATH.
  • A coding agent you use from a terminal.

How this course is set up

You choose how you follow this course:

  • On your own. Work through the text and the exercises in order.
  • With a trainer, who takes you through every step in class.
  • As reference. Come back to a page when you run into the problem it describes in your own work.

The settings for this course sit behind in the top right. Open that menu and see what is in there.

How the exercises work

During the exercises you will meet strings like {f1r5t-5t3p5}. A string in braces is called a flag. You track the flag down and type it into a box in your browser.

Hints

Every answer box has a Hint button. Click it when you are stuck.

In class, ask your trainer first, before you click Hint. Your trainer sees where the whole group is stuck and gets you moving faster than a line of text.

Icons in the text

A few icons turn up in the text as you read on.

How the workshops work

A workshop is a set of flags on the platform. Some are hidden in the code, and you let your agent find those. Others are printed by the build once the project does what the step asks. You type what you find into the platform, and the platform checks your answer straight away in the browser.

House rules

Only your agent searches. Do not read the code yourself. Ask for what you want to know and let the agent look it up.

Split the work before you prompt. "Find the flags in this repo" comes back confident and wrong. Every flag needs its own approach.

1 flag, 1 session. A search leaves a trail of dead ends behind it, and on the next flag the agent has to read past all of them. Start a fresh session for each flag unless the step says otherwise .

Let the agent run the check. A flag the agent reasons out for itself is often a plausible string with 2 letters wrong, delivered just as confidently. Have the agent run the build and print the answer. When several candidates come back that look exactly like a flag, you pick. The agent cannot tell the difference. You can.
